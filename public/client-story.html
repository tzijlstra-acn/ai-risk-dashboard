The Burning Platform

Why Act Now

Three converging forces have created a governance emergency for European banks: an AI adoption wave, a hardened regulatory environment, and a widening gap between what models do and what boards know.

The AI Proliferation Problem

Shadow AI is endemic

Business units deploy AI tools — ChatGPT plugins, Copilot integrations, specialist SaaS — without MRM review. These assets touch customer data, credit decisions, and regulated outputs. Most banks cannot enumerate them.

  • Average European bank: 80–120 AI assets. Inventoried: 30–45.
  • GenAI tools added per quarter (unregistered): 8–14
  • Shadow AI touching customer PII: estimated 40% of unregistered tools

GenAI governance gap

Traditional SR 11-7 model validation was designed for statistical models. Large language models introduce hallucination risk, prompt injection, non-determinism, and opaque data lineage — none of which existing MRM frameworks cover adequately.

  • Only 18% of banks have extended SR 11-7 to cover GenAI (EBA 2024)
  • DORA explicitly includes AI-based ICT services in scope
  • EU AI Act mandates conformity assessment for high-risk AI by Aug 2026

Compliance Perimeter

Regulatory Landscape

Six regulatory regimes apply directly to AI assets in European banking. Non-compliance is not theoretical — supervisory focus on AI is accelerating across all of them.

DORA

Digital Operational Resilience Act

EU 2022/2554 — Applies to all EU-regulated financial entities
  • Art. 28: ICT third-party register — all AI vendors must be registered
  • Concentration risk assessment for single AI model providers
  • Contractual obligations for AI subcontractors (e.g. model API providers)
  • ICT incident reporting for AI-related operational failures
  • TLPT (threat-led penetration testing) includes AI attack surfaces
Applicable since 17 Jan 2025 — enforcement active
EU AI Act

EU Artificial Intelligence Act

EU 2024/1689 — Risk-based classification framework
  • Annex III high-risk: credit scoring, fraud detection, AML screening, insurance risk
  • Conformity assessment required before deployment of high-risk AI
  • Transparency obligations for AI-generated customer-facing content
  • Prohibited practices: real-time biometric ID, social scoring (some exemptions)
  • GPAI (General Purpose AI) transparency: GPT-4, Claude, Gemini in scope
High-risk provisions: Aug 2026 deadline
SR 11-7 / EBA ML

Model Risk Management

Fed SR 11-7 + EBA Machine Learning Guidelines
  • Formal model validation for all material models (High / Medium tiers)
  • Materiality tiering: quantitative + qualitative criteria
  • Independent validation — no self-validation of production models
  • GenAI/LLM: additional robustness, hallucination, and adversarial testing
  • Model inventory: mandatory system of record with change log
Ongoing supervisory expectation — ECB TRIM active
GDPR

General Data Protection Regulation

EU 2016/679 — Data subjects' rights in automated decisions
  • Art. 22: Right not to be subject to solely automated decisions (incl. AI)
  • Explainability requirement for AI decisions affecting individuals
  • Data lineage: know where training data comes from, for how long
  • DPIA required for high-risk AI processing of personal data
  • Max fine: €20M or 4% of global annual turnover
Active — fines accelerating in 2024–25
ECOA / Reg B

Equal Credit Opportunity Act

US Regulation B — Fair lending obligations (where applicable)
  • Adverse action notice: AI credit decisions must be explainable to applicants
  • Disparate impact testing: credit models must not proxy-discriminate by race, sex, age
  • Model fairness: bias detection and monitoring required for credit AI
  • CFPB AI guidance: "complex models" require additional scrutiny
CFPB enforcement increasing — 2025 priority area
PCI-DSS v4

Payment Card Industry DSS

Version 4.0 — AI systems in payment processing scope
  • AI fraud detection systems in cardholder data environment: full PCI scope
  • ML models trained on CHD must comply with data retention + access controls
  • Requirement 6: AI-assisted code review tools in scope for software supply chain
  • Requirement 12: AI governance documentation part of compliance evidence
PCI-DSS v4 mandatory from March 2025

Why NIST AI RMF for European Banking?

While NIST is a US standard, it is the only AI risk framework adopted by major international regulators (ECB, FCA, MAS, APRA) as a reference architecture. It provides a vendor-neutral, technology-agnostic structure that maps cleanly to DORA, EU AI Act, and EBA guidelines — giving the bank a single governance spine rather than fragmented compliance silos.

Accenture has implemented NIST AI RMF across 40+ Tier 1 financial institutions in EMEA. Our accelerators compress the typical 18-month implementation to 14 weeks for the Level 3 baseline.

The new reality

  • Banks are deploying AI at 3× the rate of governance framework updates (Accenture FSI benchmark, 2024).
  • A typical Tier 2 European bank runs 80–120 distinct AI assets. Fewer than half are formally inventoried, and almost none are mapped to a foundation model.
  • GenAI tools — Copilot integrations, ChatGPT Enterprise, specialist SaaS — enter via procurement, IT shadow spend, and individual employee subscriptions, bypassing Model Risk Management entirely.
  • The first DORA enforcement dialogues in Q1 2025 centred on ICT third-party register gaps — AI and cloud-AI vendors were the single most common finding.
  • The EU AI Act's high-risk obligations bite from August 2026: credit scoring, fraud, AML and insurance pricing all fall in Annex III.

What boards are asking

  • "Do we know every AI model in production?"Usually: no.
  • "Are our credit scoring and fraud models independently validated?"Often: a backlog of 18+ months.
  • "Which GenAI tools have access to customer data?"Typically: unknown.
  • "Can we demonstrate DORA compliance on our AI ICT register to the ECB tomorrow?"Rarely: yes.
  • "Who is personally accountable if an AI decision harms a customer?"Often: undefined.

The Inventory Gap

Most banks cannot enumerate their AI assets, let alone the foundation models beneath them or the customer data they touch. You cannot govern — or defend — what you cannot see.

The Validation Backlog

GenAI has overwhelmed traditional MRM frameworks. SR 11-7 was written for statistical models — not large language models that hallucinate, drift, and cannot be back-tested conventionally.

The Regulatory Deadline

DORA is already in force. EU AI Act high-risk enforcement begins August 2026. The clock is not a future problem to schedule — it is running now, against evidence you may not yet have.

Regulatory Landscape

What Regulators Expect

Six regulatory regimes govern AI in European banking. Each has specific, enforceable AI provisions — with article numbers, deadlines, and fines — that most banks have not yet operationalised into evidence they could show a supervisor.

DORA

Digital Operational Resilience Act

Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 — ICT and third-party operational resilience for all EU financial entities.

  • Art. 28 — ICT third-party register mandatory for every AI/cloud-AI vendor
  • Art. 29 — concentration risk assessment for single-model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google)
  • Art. 30 — contractual clauses cascade to sub-outsourcers and sub-processors
  • Art. 17–23 — ICT incident classification and reporting includes AI-related failures
  • Art. 24–27 — threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) of critical AI-dependent services
Status: In force since 17 Jan 2025 — deadline passed
EU AI Act

EU Artificial Intelligence Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — risk-tiered obligations for providers and deployers of AI systems.

  • Annex III — high-risk: credit scoring, fraud detection, AML, insurance pricing
  • Art. 9 — documented risk management system across the AI lifecycle
  • Art. 43 — conformity assessment required before deployment
  • Art. 53–55 — GPAI (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini) transparency and systemic-risk duties
  • Art. 5 — prohibited practices: social scoring, untargeted biometric scraping
Status: High-risk enforcement from Aug 2026 — upcoming
SR 11-7 / EBA

Model Risk Management

Fed SR 11-7 & EBA/GL/2020/06 — supervisory expectations for model governance and validation.

  • Formal independent validation for all material models before use
  • Materiality tiering (High / Medium / Low) with defined review cadence
  • No self-validation — developers cannot validate their own production models
  • GenAI additional testing: robustness, hallucination rate, adversarial prompts
  • Full validation evidence pack retained for supervisory inspection
Status: Ongoing — ECB TRIM & internal models reviews
GDPR

General Data Protection Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 — lawful, fair and transparent processing of personal data.

  • Art. 22 — right not to be subject to solely automated decisions
  • Art. 13–15 — meaningful information about the logic of AI decisions
  • Art. 35 — DPIA required for high-risk AI processing personal data
  • Art. 30 — records of processing activities for every AI use case
  • Max fine: €20M or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher
Status: Enforcement accelerating on AI/profiling
ECOA / Reg B

Equal Credit Opportunity Act

15 U.S.C. §1691 / 12 CFR 1002 — non-discrimination in credit; mirrored by EU/FCA fair-lending duties.

  • Disparate-impact testing — credit models must not proxy-discriminate
  • Adverse-action notices must give specific, accurate reasons (CFPB Circular 2022-03)
  • Continuous bias monitoring across protected classes
  • "Black-box" complexity is not a defence for unexplained denials
  • Model documentation sufficient to reconstruct any decision
Status: CFPB & FCA Consumer Duty 2025 priority
PCI-DSS v4.0

Payment Card Industry DSS

PCI-DSS v4.0.1 — security of the cardholder data environment (CDE).

  • AI fraud detection touching the CDE falls fully in PCI scope
  • Req. 3 — ML models trained on CHD inherit retention & protection duties
  • Req. 6 — AI-assisted development tools in scope for secure SDLC
  • Req. 7–8 — least-privilege access to AI systems and training data
  • Quarterly scanning and change control on AI components in the CDE
Status: v4.0 mandatory since 31 Mar 2025

The Framework

What AI Risk Management Is

Before the maturity path, a shared definition. Accenture builds every AI Risk Management programme on the NIST AI RMF — the only framework major regulators (ECB, FCA, MAS, APRA) consistently reference as an architecture, and the one that maps cleanly onto EU banking law.

Definition

AI Risk Management is the systematic process of identifying, assessing, and governing the risks introduced by artificial intelligence systems across their full lifecycle — from design through deployment, monitoring, and retirement. It encompasses model risk, data risk, vendor and concentration risk, operational resilience, and regulatory compliance — under a single, accountable, auditable operating model.

Client Prerequisites

What It Requires From You

A Level 3 delivery is a partnership. To reach a defensible posture in 14 weeks, we need defined access, executive sponsorship, and a willingness to formalise AI ownership. Here is exactly what that means — and who owns each step.

Data & Systems

  • Access to existing MRM system of record (Archer, MetricStream, ServiceNow GRC)
  • Network access logs or cloud access broker (CASB) data for shadow AI detection
  • Vendor contracts and ICT service agreements for DORA mapping
  • Model documentation library (or acknowledgment that one doesn't exist)
  • List of business owners per AI asset (or a process to identify them)

Organisational

  • Executive sponsor: CRO or equivalent (minimum Senior Director level)
  • MRM team access for validation status and history
  • Procurement team for vendor AI spend analysis
  • Compliance team for regulatory mapping review
  • IT / Security team for SIEM / CASB data access

Change Management

  • Willingness to formalise AI asset ownership (business owners named per model)
  • Approval of AI Acceptable Use Policy (or update to existing)
  • 3LoD governance buy-in (1LoD business; 2LoD MRM/Compliance; 3LoD Internal Audit)
  • Board / ExCo awareness session (Accenture-led, included in scope)
  • Agreement to a single decision-maker for sign-off gates
